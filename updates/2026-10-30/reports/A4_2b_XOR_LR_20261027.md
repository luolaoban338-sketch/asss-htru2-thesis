# A4 XOR Logistic Regression Method Validation

English research progress report | 27 October 2026 | A4 2b LR half

The authorized logistic regression half of the XOR package completed eight independent generated datasets and 48 nested selectors. For CSR 111 at the prespecified size two, mean joint recovery of both signal variables was 0.083333 and mean test F1 was 0.494482. All prescribed variants and all eight datasets are retained. Together with the sealed RF half and verified shared data and partitions, these results complete the prescribed RF and LR layers of A4.2b. They do not complete the wider A4 stage.

## Frozen design

Each dataset has six independent standard normal predictors, 600 training rows and 2000 independently generated test rows. The binary outcome is the XOR of the signs of X1 and X2, with independent label flips of probability 0.05. X3 through X6 are noise. Joint recovery is the indicator that a selected set contains both X1 and X2, averaged over nested resamples.

The fixed logistic regression uses L2 regularization with C=1, an intercept, the lbfgs solver, tolerance 0.000001 and at most 5000 iterations. No interaction terms or hyperparameter search were added after observing XOR outcomes. Each of six selector resamples has 480 training rows; four contribution samples of 384 rows each use two utility folds. All final models use the same selector rows and probability threshold 0.5.

C denotes Monte Carlo variance correction, S the stability penalty and R the redundancy penalty. Selection uses B=4, M=64, lambda=0.5 and eta=0.1. All eight CSR variants, k=1/2/3, a random feature path and all six features remain recorded. Structural aliases 001=101 and 000=100 remain visible. Test data and exact diagnostics are accessed only after selection commitments are saved and hashed.

## Interpretation and remaining work

This is a descriptive synthetic diagnostic for one XOR generator, two prespecified learner configurations and eight independent datasets. Null, mixed and adverse outcomes remain evidence. Results cannot establish overall superiority, cross-dataset robustness, noninferiority, deployment readiness or general scalability.
